# FFHub interview prep — 4 August 2026

Prepared from BoxBox's official fantasy totals for the 11 completed Grands Prix,
round-level scoring components, price history, and the current pre-FP/horizon models.

## The five numbers worth remembering

1. **Kimi Antonelli: 429 fantasy points.** That is 85 more than Hamilton in second.
   He has scored 20+ in 10 of 11 weekends, has no retirement, and started at only
   £23.2M. His 60 fastest-lap points and 100 qualifying points explain much of the edge.
2. **Lewis Hamilton: the safest premium.** He is the only premium with no negative
   weekend and no retirement. He has also scored 20+ in 10 of 11 rounds, with the
   lowest volatility among the leading seven drivers (11.6-point standard deviation).
3. **Isack Hadjar: the biggest turnaround.** His average rose from 1.2 over his first
   five completed races to 22.6 over his last five. Over the last three, he has averaged
   25.3 versus Verstappen's 20.7 in the same team.
4. **Sprint weekends have been worth about 70% more driver points.** The four completed
   sprints averaged 340 total driver fantasy points versus 200 on normal weekends. The
   best driver averaged 59.5 on sprint weekends versus 47.6 otherwise — a 25% higher
   multiplier-chip ceiling.
5. **Small price rises are option value, not banked points.** BoxBox's season simulation
   values a forecast +£0.3M rise at about 1.4 future points with the present horizon.
   An extra £0.3M changed the continuation lineup in none of eight genuine deadline
   states, whereas £1M unlocked a better lineup in 87.5% of them.

## Season-so-far talking points

### Strategy and biggest lesson

> “The biggest lesson is that budget only matters when it crosses an affordability
> threshold. I do not want to give away ten real points just to chase a routine £0.3M
> rise. Our simulation currently values that forecast rise at only about 1.4 future
> points. Build budget early, but later in the season convert it into points.”

Useful follow-up: two simulated managers finished with the same £124.3M team value but
150 points apart. Having spending power and deploying it well are different skills.

### Chip success and failure — use only if it matches your real team

- **3x success benchmark:** Kimi's 68 in China was the best driver round. A 3x there
  added 68 points over the normal 2x captain score. His 62 in Canada was second.
- **The Monaco Limitless lesson:** in perfect hindsight, removing the £100M cap was
  worth only 18 points at Monaco, the smallest uplift of any completed round. At Miami
  it was worth 167. The reason is that Monaco's best scorers included affordable names
  such as Gasly, Hadjar and Lindblad. “Premium track” did not mean “premium assets won.”
- **No Negative hindsight:** the largest all-asset negative damage came in Australia
  (146 points), Barcelona (135) and Monaco (128). It should be triggered by forecast
  incident/weather/reliability risk, not merely because a weekend contains a Sprint.

### Reliability as the season's hidden currency

There have been 48 DNF/DSQ/DNS results across 242 car-starts: 4.36 per race, or roughly
19.8% attrition. Kimi and Hamilton have zero. Norris and Piastri have three each, costing
each 60 fantasy points in retirement penalties alone. That contrast explains a large
part of the premium hierarchy.

## Asset analysis

### One asset that has impressed: Hamilton, with Kimi as the obvious headline

Kimi is the largest scorer, but Hamilton makes the more interesting interview answer:

> “Kimi is obviously the asset of the season, but Hamilton has impressed me most as a
> fantasy asset. He has no negative score, no retirement and ten 20-point weekends out
> of eleven. His average is 31.3 with much less volatility than the other premiums. In
> a year of reliability shocks, his floor has been as valuable as anyone's ceiling.”

Alternative constructor answer:

> “Racing Bulls has been the great budget constructor story: 344 points and a £6.6M
> rise from £6.3M to £12.9M. Even at today's price it has returned 26.7 season points
> per current £M — almost level with Mercedes and Ferrari.”

### One asset to watch: Norris

> “Norris is my premium watchlist name. His first-five average was 19.0; his last-five
> average is 29.8; and he has scored 115 across the last three rounds. Over those same
> three, Piastri scored 27. Norris is still £1.1M cheaper than at the start, and our
> six-round model has him second only to Kimi.”

### The mid-price watch: Hadjar — but do not chase blindly

Hadjar has the largest improvement in the field: +21.4 points per race when comparing
the first five with the last five. His recent three-race average is also 4.7 higher than
Verstappen's. But BoxBox's forward prior is nearer 9.7 per race for R14-R19. The smart
line is: **watch the FP evidence; do not automatically extrapolate the hot streak.**

### The fantasy-only gem: Ocon

Ocon is 17th in the real championship on three points, but 12th in fantasy on 120. He
has never retired or posted a negative fantasy weekend, and 70 of his 120 points came
from overtakes — second only to Verstappen's 75. He is the cleanest example of why F1
standings and F1 Fantasy value are not the same thing.

### “Thank you and sell” candidate: Colapinto

Colapinto produced 123 points, no negative weekend, no retirement, and the largest
driver price gain: +£5.0M. But his price is now £11.2M and the R14-R19 model returns only
3.2 points in total. He has been a superb budget builder; the interesting question is
whether managers are holding the story after the value has already been captured.

### Biggest disappointment at the current price: Piastri

Piastri has 160 points, three retirements, three negative weekends and only three
20-point rounds. He costs £24.4M — almost Hamilton/Leclerc money — yet the current
six-round prior gives him 55.8 points versus Hamilton's 145.4, Leclerc's 134.2 and
Norris's 167.2. Norris leads him by 67 for the season and 88 over the last three races.

### Constructor watch: Audi

Audi averaged only 0.6 points over its first five completed races but 16.4 over its last
five. At £6.8M it is the current low-cost forward watch: the R14-R19 model has 57.8
points, better value than the other budget constructors. Racing Bulls remains the
season's value success, but the priors expect its performance to fall sharply after
Zandvoort.

## Pivotal moments

- **China:** highest individual driver score of the season, Kimi 68, and the cleanest
  3x benchmark.
- **Miami:** Norris scored 54 and the perfect-hindsight value of Limitless was 167,
  the highest of the year.
- **Monaco:** only 122 fantasy points across all 22 drivers, ten assets scored negative,
  and Limitless had only 18 points of theoretical budget-cap value. This was the round
  that punished “premium circuit = buy every premium” thinking.
- **Barcelona:** Hamilton scored 56 while the field absorbed 135 points of negative
  damage. It was a huge separator between reliable and fragile lineups.
- **Silverstone:** the opposite kind of pivotal round — 384 driver points and only 23
  points of negative damage. Positive multipliers mattered more than protection.
- **The Norris/Hadjar turn:** Norris has 115 over the last three; Hadjar has 76 and has
  outscored Verstappen's 62. The first-half hierarchy is not the current hierarchy.

## Second-half opportunities

### The two remaining Sprint weekends

Completed Sprint weekends generated 70% more total driver points and a 25% higher best-
driver ceiling. That supports saving **positive multiplier chips** for a Sprint. It does
not automatically make a Sprint the best No Negative or Limitless round.

The current priors make Singapore the bigger multiplier opportunity: Kimi projects at
50.6 there versus 30.5 at Zandvoort. That is a priors-only forecast, so the practical
decision is to use Zandvoort's FP1 or wait for Singapore rather than locking the chip on
calendar type alone.

### Madrid

BoxBox's circuit vector currently treats Madrid as a balanced layout: overtaking 5/10,
average corner speed 6/10, straight-line importance 6/10 and downforce 6/10, with a
higher 7/10 Turn 1 incident risk. The interview answer:

> “For a debut circuit I want optionality. Hold transfers, avoid committing a chip on
> track reputation, and let FP supply the first real comparable evidence. Repeatable
> three- and five-lap pace matters more than a one-off purple lap, and long-run evidence
> should be allowed to stay missing rather than pretending every driver completed a
> representative run.”

### Malaysia / Sepang

F1 and the FIA have confirmed that Sepang will host the 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix on
2-4 October, between Baku and Singapore. The official calendar now contains 23 rounds,
so there are **12 remaining races, not 11**, after Hungary. It also creates a three-race
Baku-Sepang-Singapore run and adds a historically weather-sensitive venue immediately
before a Sprint street circuit. Preserve transfer flexibility and do not commit all
remaining chips before that sequence is understood.

BoxBox's current seed and horizon do not yet include Sepang, so no Malaysia projection
should be quoted as model output.

### Calendar changes and the points-to-budget exchange rate

The marginal-budget study fits a saturating curve:
**8.57 × (1 - exp(-races remaining / 1.80))** future projected points per secured
£1M, followed by separate discounts for forecast price realisation and realised
marginal points. A forecast rise becomes spendable one race later.

| Calendar case | Races left | Forecast +£0.3M | Secured +£1.0M |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Old working horizon | 11 | 1.39 pts | 5.34 pts |
| Confirmed calendar with Malaysia | 12 | 1.39 pts | 5.35 pts |
| True extra race on top | 13 | 1.39 pts | 5.35 pts |

The headline is that Malaysia does **not** materially increase the present exchange
rate. At this long horizon the curve is already almost flat: moving from 11 to 12
races raises the decision value of a secured £1M by only about 0.01 point, while a
forecast +£0.3M still rounds to 1.39 points. A forecast rise still needs to be roughly
£2.16M to justify giving up 10 certain points under the smooth model.

> “Malaysia gives us another scoring and chip opportunity, but it does not suddenly
> make routine price rises more valuable. At 12 races left, a forecast +£0.3M is still
> worth only about 1.4 future points. The extra date matters more for chip timing and
> transfer flexibility than for the budget exchange rate.”

#### How to frame the Imola rumour

Imola is being discussed as a contingency finale if the Middle East ending changes.
Current reporting does not establish it as a 24th race: it could replace Abu Dhabi
one-for-one, or become one replacement if both Qatar and Abu Dhabi are lost. In the
first case the horizon stays at 12 races; in the second it falls back to 11. Only a
genuine additional Imola round on top of the current 23 would create a 13-race horizon
- and even then the current forecast +£0.3M value still rounds to 1.39 points.

The calendar effect becomes more important later. With three races left, a forecast
+£0.3M is worth about 0.93 point; adding one genuinely usable race lifts that to about
1.13. Recalculate once the finale is confirmed rather than treating today's 12-race
assumption as permanent.

### Where is the edge?

> “The edge is in separating signal from story. Use repeatable FP pace rather than the
> fastest single lap, model DNF risk instead of calling every retirement random, and
> price budget as an option rather than as points. Then be willing to sell the asset
> that made you money when the forward value has disappeared.”
